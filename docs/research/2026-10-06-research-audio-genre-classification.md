# Free models classify genre; Spotify withholds audio

Yes, software that infers genre from the waveform exists, and the strongest free option is good enough to use as a fallback. MTG's **Discogs-EffNet** model predicts **400 Discogs styles** from audio, ships as an **18 MB ONNX file plus a 2 MB classifier head**, and can run inside Spotify's Chromium renderer through onnxruntime-web ([Essentia models](https://essentia.upf.edu/models.html); [discogs-effnet metadata](https://essentia.upf.edu/models/feature-extractors/discogs-effnet/discogs-effnet-bs64-1.json)). Of your two claims, the first mostly holds: the only audio-analysing vendor that gives a self-serve free API key (Cyanite) caps it at about **5 tracks a month**, and every other vendor is behind sales, paid, or gone ([Cyanite FAQ](https://cyanite.ai/faq/)). The second claim does not hold as stated. Free open models are the research state of the art, but no system, free or paid, reliably assigns one niche style to one track, because the task itself is ambiguous. The hard constraint is not model quality but audio access. A Spicetify extension cannot read decoded Spotify audio, and Spotify's terms forbid feeding Spotify content into an ML model ([Spotify User Guidelines](https://www.spotify.com/us/legal/user-guidelines/)). The workable input is the **30-second preview** that Apple and Deezer serve with open CORS headers. The recommended design keeps MusicBrainz first, adds cheap metadata fallbacks, and runs Discogs-EffNet on a store preview only when metadata comes back empty. The weights are downloaded at runtime under their own non-commercial licence, and the results are shown as top-3 styles with confidence scores.

## Claim one holds; claim two confuses task ceiling with model quality

Claim (1), that third-party resources sit behind a paid or sales-gated API, is **largely true for audio-based genre at any useful volume**. Access itself is not walled off, because self-serve paid APIs exist: Replicate hosts MTG's Discogs-EffNet at about **$0.00022 per track** (about 4,545 runs per dollar) ([Replicate mtg/effnet-discogs](https://replicate.com/mtg/effnet-discogs)). What is walled off is the *free* route. Hugging Face's free hosted inference no longer has any provider that supports `audio-classification` ([HF audio classification task](https://huggingface.co/docs/inference-providers/tasks/audio-classification)), and Replicate requires billing once its trial ends ([Replicate billing](https://replicate.com/docs/topics/billing)). The free APIs that do respond (AudD, ACRCloud, Shazam wrappers, iTunes, Deezer) return genre from **catalogue metadata, not from audio**, so they fail on exactly the obscure tracks you care about.

Claim (2), that free resources are not competent, is **refuted for coarse genres and confirmed for niche styles, but the second part applies to paid services too**. On the 10-genre GTZAN benchmark, linear probes on free models reach **84–92%** (MERT-v2 91.7%, MusicFM 84.1%, MuQ 83.8%) ([HF MERT-v2-30s](https://huggingface.co/m-a-p/MERT-v2-30s)). Dataset errors cap a perfect system at about **94.5%** ([Sturm, arXiv 1306.1461](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.1461)). On fine-grained taxonomies, precision collapses for everyone. Discogs-EffNet scores ROC-AUC 0.954 but **PR-AUC 0.206** across 400 styles ([discogs-effnet metadata](https://essentia.upf.edu/models/feature-extractors/discogs-effnet/discogs-effnet-bs64-1.json)), and MAEST-30s reaches only **21.6 mAP** on the 87 MTG-Jamendo genres ([MAEST, arXiv 2309.16418](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.16418)). Paid services do not escape this. A small vendor-blog test labelled Fela Kuti's "Water No Get Enemy" as "Afrobeat" (Bridge), "Funk/Jazz" (Cyanite) and "Latin" (AIMS), and AIMS tagged Portuguese fado as "Klezmer" ([Soundcharts](https://soundcharts.com/fr/blog/ai-music-analysis-2026)). That test covered five tracks and is not independent, and no independent benchmark of commercial genre APIs was found. MetaBrainz shut down AcousticBrainz partly because its audio genre models "didn't work very well" ([MetaBrainz blog, archived](https://gwern.net/doc/www/blog.metabrainz.org/54a8eae256b311a8a14cce1195ca19e27dd1f298.html)). That failure came from older SVM models, not from today's CNN and transformer models.

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence in one line |
|---|---|---|
| (1) Third-party audio genre is walled behind paid/sales APIs | **Mostly true** | One free self-serve key (Cyanite, ~5/month); HF free route closed; Replicate cheap but paid |
| (2) Free resources are not competent | **False for coarse genre, true for single niche labels, and equally true for paid** | Free models score 84–92% on GTZAN; everyone is weak on 400-style precision |

## Discogs-EffNet tags 400 styles in 20 MB, under a non-commercial licence

The open-source landscape splits along licence lines. The only open models with **fine-grained style output** come from the Music Technology Group (MTG) in Barcelona. Discogs-EffNet and MAEST predict 400 Discogs styles (519 for one MAEST variant), and a separate head covers 87 MTG-Jamendo genres. **All MTG weights are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, and the Essentia library, essentia.js and MAEST code are **AGPL-3.0** ([MTG/essentia-models LICENSE](https://github.com/MTG/essentia-models/blob/master/LICENSE); [Essentia models](https://essentia.upf.edu/models.html)). The permissively licensed models are coarser. AST (BSD-3), PANNs (code MIT, weights CC BY 4.0) and YAMNet (Apache-2.0) predict AudioSet classes, whose "Music genre" branch has about **52 usable labels** ([AudioSet ontology](https://github.com/audioset/ontology)). LAION-CLAP (code CC0, weights Apache-2.0) scores audio against any text label list. Its best music checkpoint reaches **71% zero-shot on GTZAN** ([LAION-AI/CLAP](https://github.com/LAION-AI/CLAP)); a mirror of the repo reports 73% for a different checkpoint. The strongest music foundation models, MERT and MuQ, are **CC BY-NC 4.0**, produce only embeddings, and have no JavaScript port ([HF MERT-v1-330M](https://huggingface.co/m-a-p/MERT-v1-330M); [tencent-ailab/MuQ](https://github.com/tencent-ailab/MuQ)).

| Model | Output | Licence (weights) | Browser-ready size | Fit for the extension |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Discogs-EffNet + genre_discogs400** | 400 Discogs styles | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | ~18 MB ONNX + ~2 MB head (.pb) | **Best**: fine-grained, small |
| MAEST-30s (519l) | 519 styles | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | ~348 MB ONNX | Too heavy for a fallback |
| LAION-CLAP (Xenova port) | Zero-shot over any labels | Apache-2.0 | 34 MB quantized audio encoder | Permissive alternative, moderate quality |
| AST AudioSet (Xenova port) | ~52 genre labels | BSD-3-Clause | 51–90 MB quantized | Coarse |
| GTZAN fine-tunes (HuBERT ONNX etc.) | 10 genres | mostly Apache-2.0 | varies | Too coarse for obscure tracks |
| MERT, MuQ, MusicFM | Embeddings only | NC (MusicFM: MIT) | No JS port | Not usable in-browser |

Sizes come from the Essentia model directories and the Hugging Face file listings ([discogs-effnet dir](https://essentia.upf.edu/models/feature-extractors/discogs-effnet/); [Xenova/clap-htsat-unfused](https://huggingface.co/Xenova/clap-htsat-unfused); [Xenova AST](https://huggingface.co/Xenova/ast-finetuned-audioset-10-10-0.4593)). The supporting runtimes are active and permissive: onnxruntime-web is MIT (1.30.0, September 2026), and transformers.js is Apache-2.0 ([microsoft/onnxruntime](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime); [huggingface/transformers.js](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers.js)). essentia.js, which would supply the matching audio preprocessing, is stale: npm 0.1.3 has not been updated since 2021–22 ([npm essentia.js](https://www.npmjs.com/package/essentia.js)). Two engineering gaps remain open. No ONNX export of the genre_discogs400 head for EffNet was found (only the 2 MB `.pb`, while the MAEST heads ship ONNX), and the 16 kHz mel-spectrogram front-end has to be reimplemented in TypeScript to match Essentia's preprocessing exactly. Neither problem is hard, but neither has been verified. No measured browser inference time exists for any of these models.

## Only Cyanite and Gemini hand out a free key that listens to audio

Requirement (b) asks for a service that analyses audio well, issues a key from a free account, and costs nothing. Two qualify. **Cyanite** is the only dedicated music-analysis vendor found that does this: "any free account is eligible for API integrations", private users get **5 songs per month**, and inputs include file uploads and Spotify track IDs, for which it analyses the 30-second preview ([Cyanite FAQ](https://cyanite.ai/faq/); [Cyanite API blog](https://cyanite.ai/blog/music-analysis-api/)). Production API use costs **€290/month** plus usage, and setting up an integration asks for a webhook endpoint, which a browser extension cannot easily host ([Cyanite integration docs](https://api-docs.cyanite.ai/docs/create-integration)). The second is **Google's Gemini API**, covered in the AI section below. Every other candidate fails at least one condition, as the table shows.

| Service | Analyses audio? | Free self-serve key? | Why it does or doesn't fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cyanite** | Yes (genre + subgenre) | **Yes, ~5 tracks/month** | Works as a "classify this one track" button with your own key; webhook setup adds friction |
| **Gemini API (Flash)** | Yes (general audio LLM) | **Yes** | Free tier exists, but quotas are small and free-tier data is used for training |
| Bridge.audio | Yes (839 tags) | Web app only; API by demo | Sales-gated ([Bridge.audio API](https://www.bridge.audio/features/api/)) |
| AIMS, Sonoteller | Yes | No (demo or email) | Sales-gated ([A2IM](https://a2im.org/a2im-am-spotlight-aims-api); [sonoteller.ai](https://sonoteller.ai/)) |
| Musiio | — | — | Acquired by SoundCloud in 2022; site redirects |
| ReccoBeats | Features only | Free | Returns 9 Spotify-style features, **no genre** ([ReccoBeats docs](https://reccobeats.com/docs/documentation/Analysis/audio-features-extraction)) |
| Replicate (MTG models) | Yes (400 styles) | Trial, then billing | Cheapest competent option, but paid |
| AudD, ACRCloud, Shazam | Fingerprint only | Trial quotas | Genre comes from catalogue metadata; unknown tracks get nothing ([AudD docs](https://docs.audd.io/)) |
| Spotify audio-features | Was audio-derived | Closed to new apps since 2024-11-27 | Never had track genre ([Spotify dev blog](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2024-11-27-changes-to-the-web-api)) |

Because the extension is open source, any key must be **bring-your-own-key**, entered by each user in settings. A shared key embedded in public code would leak. Free metadata APIs deserve a place in the chain even though they do not analyse audio. Last.fm (free key, CORS-open), iTunes `primaryGenreName` and Deezer album genres are free and fast. They are also coarse: iTunes files the Fela Kuti track under "Worldwide", and Deezer returns no genres for that album ([iTunes Search](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=water+no+get+enemy+fela&entity=song&limit=1); [Deezer album](https://api.deezer.com/album/7113170)).

## Local Essentia taggers exist, but none listens to Spotify

For requirement (c), several free tools on the desktop analyse audio files locally, and all of the good ones are built on the same MTG models. **Essentia-to-Metadata** (MIT, active in 2026) classifies 400 Discogs genres fully offline, writes file tags, and integrates with Picard ([WB2024/Essentia-to-Metadata](https://github.com/WB2024/Essentia-to-Metadata)). **essentia-autotagger** (MIT, v0.1.0, June 2026) adds thresholds and a JSON report of predictions with confidences ([cookiephone/essentia-autotagger](https://github.com/cookiephone/essentia-autotagger)). **settag** (AGPL-3.0, September 2026) uses MAEST in a TUI and a CLI ([lsdcapital/settag](https://github.com/lsdcapital/settag)). The most mature self-hosted server, **AudioMuse-AI** (AGPL-3.0, v3.6.3, September 2026), runs MusiCNN and CLAP through ONNX Runtime, exposes a REST API on `127.0.0.1:8000`, and ships native Windows, macOS and Linux packages ([NeptuneHub/AudioMuse-AI](https://github.com/NeptuneHub/AudioMuse-AI)). Its label set is the 50 coarse MSD/Last.fm tags, however, and it keys results to media-server items rather than arbitrary audio. The older beets route (**beets-xtractor**) uses Essentia's deprecated SVM models, which are licensed CC BY-NC-ND ([Essentia SVM models](https://essentia.upf.edu/gaia_svm_models.html)). Popular players and DJ tools such as Mixxx, Strawberry, OneTagger and bliss-rs compute beats, key, similarity or fingerprints, **not genre** ([Mixxx analyzers](https://github.com/mixxxdj/mixxx/tree/main/src/analyzer); [OneTagger](https://github.com/Marekkon5/onetagger)).

None of these tools solves the Spotify case, because none has access to the stream. Searches found **no open-source project that captures Spotify playback and classifies its genre**. Existing Spicetify genre extensions are metadata-only ([LucasOe/spicetify-genres](https://github.com/LucasOe/spicetify-genres)). A local helper that records system audio and calls Essentia would be technically novel, but the next two sections explain why it is the wrong path. These tools remain useful in two ways: as reference implementations of the preprocessing and inference code, and for Spotify **local files** (which the extension currently skips via `is_local`), whose audio sits on the user's own disk.

## Audio LLMs top GTZAN and fall to near-random on subgenres

For requirement (d), **Gemini is the only major hosted LLM API with a free tier that accepts raw audio**. The October 2026 pricing page lists free input for the Gemini 3.x Flash and Flash-Lite models and for 2.5 Flash. Pro models are paid-only, and free-tier content is "used to improve our products" ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). A 30-second clip costs about **960 tokens** (32 tokens per second), so the binding limit is requests per day. Google no longer publishes those limits ([Gemini audio docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/audio); [Gemini rate limits](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits)), and secondary sources report that the 2.5 Flash free tier was cut to **20 requests per day** in December 2025 ([agentdeals.dev](https://agentdeals.dev/gemini-api-pricing-changes)). OpenAI's audio models have no free tier ([OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)). Claude's API documents no audio input ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)). Mistral's Voxtral handles speech, not music ([Mistral audio docs](https://docs.mistral.ai/capabilities/audio/)). Alibaba's Qwen-Omni quota is a one-time 90-day grant ([Alibaba free quota](https://help.aliyun.com/en/model-studio/new-free-quota)).

The benchmarks argue against making an LLM the primary classifier. On coarse GTZAN, the newest large models match the specialist models: Qwen3-Omni-30B (Apache-2.0) self-reports **93.1%**, and NVIDIA's Music Flamingo (non-commercial licence) reports **84.45%** ([Qwen3-Omni card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct); [Music Flamingo, arXiv 2511.10289](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10289v1)). On fine-grained genre the picture reverses. CMI-Bench (ISMIR 2025) found the best open audio LLM at **ROC-AUC 66 on MTG-Jamendo genre, against 88 for supervised models**, with world and traditional genres "consistently below average" and results that change sharply with prompt wording ([CMI-Bench, arXiv 2506.12285](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12285v1)). No Gemini 3.x score on a genre benchmark was found. Running a strong model yourself means GPU hardware: Qwen3-Omni needs about **79 GB of VRAM** in BF16 ([Qwen3-Omni card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct)). llama.cpp's audio support for smaller models (Qwen2.5-Omni 3B/7B) is labelled "highly experimental" ([llama.cpp multimodal](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multimodal.md)). For obscure tracks there is one more trap: an LLM given the title and artist will guess from what it knows about the artist, so it should receive audio only, plus a closed list of labels to choose from.

## Spotify audio is off-limits; store previews are the legal input

The extension has **no practical way to read decoded Spotify audio**. `Spicetify.getAudioData()` never returned samples. It fetched Spotify's precomputed analysis JSON ([spicetify/cli helpers.js](https://github.com/spicetify/cli/blob/8c92e3067de9659caca4c9da7979da3897458e27/src/jsHelper/spicetifyWrapper/helpers.js)), and that endpoint now fails with "Resolver not found!" on Spotify 1.3.x ([spicetify-winamp-classic#11](https://github.com/afonsoamaro/spicetify-winamp-classic/issues/11); [spicetify-apps-dist#94](https://github.com/Pithaya/spicetify-apps-dist/issues/94)). Playback is decoded in Spotify's native core and protected by Widevine, so the renderer has no media element to capture. This is inferred from local inspection of the client; no official Spotify document describes the pipeline. System-audio loopback through a native helper would work technically, but it conflicts directly with Spotify's terms. The User Guidelines prohibit "ripping, recording" and "ingesting Spotify Content into a machine learning or AI model" ([Spotify User Guidelines](https://www.spotify.com/us/legal/user-guidelines/)), and the Developer Terms contain the same ML clause ([Spotify Developer Terms](https://developer.spotify.com/terms)). The wording covers inference as well as training, so analysing a few seconds in memory does not avoid it.

**Apple and Deezer 30-second previews** avoid Spotify content entirely. Local checks from this machine found that the iTunes Search API and its preview CDN both send `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`. Deezer resolves a track by ISRC without a key and its preview CDN is CORS-open, but its JSON API sends no allow-origin header, and its preview URLs are signed and expire ([iTunes Search](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=radiohead+creep&entity=song&limit=1); [Deezer track by ISRC](https://api.deezer.com/track/isrc:GBAYE9200070)). A 30-second clip suits classifiers trained on 3–30-second patches. Three caveats apply. First, the repo's own code states that **Spotify's page CSP blocks direct cross-origin fetches**, which is why MusicBrainz calls go through the Spicetify CORS proxy (`module/mod.tsx`, `resolveMusicBrainzFetch`). That conflicts with the research finding that xpui's `index.html` has no CSP meta tag, which suggests the policy is set by response headers. Preview audio (~480 KB per clip) and model downloads would therefore need the proxy chain or an allowed origin. Pushing that traffic through a hosted proxy raises bandwidth and courtesy concerns. Second, no figures exist on how many obscure tracks have Apple or Deezer previews. Tracks released through a distributor usually reach every store, but tracks missing from MusicBrainz are not guaranteed to be there. Third, Apple's and Deezer's terms on programmatic use of previews were not reviewed.

## Licences push the model out of the MIT bundle

The licence picture is workable for a free extension, provided the parts stay separate. CC BY-NC-SA permits non-commercial use with attribution. A free, non-monetised extension is plausibly non-commercial. MTG has made no statement on browser extensions, and donations or sponsorship could blur the line, so this is a reading of the licence text, not legal advice ([MTG/essentia-models LICENSE](https://github.com/MTG/essentia-models/blob/master/LICENSE)). The weights **cannot be relicensed as MIT**. Any fine-tuned derivative inherits the share-alike terms, and a commercial fork would lose the right to use them. The clean pattern is to **download the weights at runtime** from essentia.upf.edu, cache them, show the CC BY-NC-SA notice and credit MTG in the UI and README, and keep them out of the git repo. The code dependency matters just as much. Bundling **essentia.js (AGPL-3.0)** would make the distributed bundle effectively AGPL ([MTG/essentia.js](https://github.com/MTG/essentia.js)), whereas **onnxruntime-web (MIT)** plus a hand-written mel front-end keeps the extension fully MIT. For a variant with no non-commercial strings at all, the fallback is LAION-CLAP (Apache-2.0 weights) or AST (BSD-3), at the cost of coarser labels. Uploading audio to Gemini's free tier adds a separate obligation: users must be told that Google may use the clips, because free-tier content is used for training ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)).

## Run a metadata-first chain with a 20 MB audio fallback

The extension resolves each genre to a Spotify "The Sound of &lt;genre&gt;" playlist, so whatever label the audio model produces has to match Spotify's genre vocabulary. Discogs styles arrive as `Electronic---Darkwave`. Splitting on `---` and lowercasing gives the style name ("darkwave"), which overlaps heavily with Spotify's old Every Noise taxonomy of 6,291 genres ([MusicGenreDB](https://github.com/ThatSINEWAVE/MusicGenreDB)). The overlap is an inference; nobody has measured it. Given the precision ceiling, the UI should show the **top 2–3 styles above a confidence threshold**, mark them as inferred from audio, and fall back to the Discogs parent genre when no style clears the threshold.

| Step | Source | Cost / key | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Per-track cache (track URI → genres + source + confidence) | Free | Classify each track once |
| 1 | MusicBrainz recording tags, then artist tags (current) | Free | Keep as primary |
| 2 | Last.fm track/artist top tags | Free user key (BYOK) | Best metadata coverage for indie tracks; CORS-open |
| 3 | Deezer album genres / iTunes `primaryGenreName` | Free, keyless | Coarse; same calls also find the preview URL |
| 4 | **Discogs-EffNet on Apple/Deezer preview**, in a Web Worker via onnxruntime-web | Free; ~20 MB one-time download | Opt-in setting; CC BY-NC-SA notice; top-k with threshold |
| 5 (optional) | Gemini Flash free tier or Cyanite, BYOK, audio only, closed label list | Free with small quotas | Manual "re-classify" button, not automatic |
| — | Not recommended: loopback capture, `getAudioData`, yt-dlp | — | Spotify/YouTube terms, dead endpoints, native helpers |

The concrete next steps, in order, are as follows.

| # | Task | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In Spotify devtools, probe the runtime: `fetch` to the iTunes preview CDN, essentia.upf.edu and huggingface.co (direct and via `client.corsProxy`); `WebAssembly.instantiate`; Blob-URL `Worker`; `crossOriginIsolated`; `navigator.gpu` | Every network path and WASM/worker capability has been tested in the real client |
| 2 | Measure preview coverage: for 50–100 tracks where MusicBrainz returns nothing today, check iTunes Search and Deezer ISRC for a preview URL | Hit rate known; if it is low, the audio fallback is not worth building |
| 3 | Spike the front-end: port Essentia's 16 kHz mel preprocessing for Discogs-EffNet to TS, and compare embeddings against Python `essentia-tensorflow` on 20 clips | Embeddings match within tolerance |
| 4 | Convert the 2 MB `genre_discogs400-discogs-effnet-1.pb` head to ONNX (or check for an existing export) | Embedding → 400 sigmoid outputs works in onnxruntime-web; inference time per clip recorded |
| 5 | Build a Discogs-style → Spotify-genre mapping, and test that "The Sound of &lt;style&gt;" resolves for the top 400 styles | Hit rate per style known; misses fall back to the parent genre |
| 6 | Evaluate on your own obscure tracks: hand-label ~50, compare EffNet, CLAP (zero-shot over your genre vocabulary) and Gemini Flash | Pick the default model and thresholds on real data, not GTZAN |
| 7 | Ship behind an opt-in setting, with runtime model download, Cache Storage, the licence notice, and per-track result caching | README states the licence split and the data each provider receives |

## Conclusion

The research shifts the problem from "is there a competent free classifier" to "is there legal audio to classify". The classifier exists, is small, and runs in the client. The bottleneck is whether Apple or Deezer hold a preview for the tracks that MusicBrainz misses, and step 2 measures that cheaply before any model work begins. If coverage is poor, no audio approach inside the rules will help, and effort is better spent on metadata breadth (Last.fm, Discogs styles, Wikidata).

The second shift concerns what "competent" should mean for this feature. Every system, paid or free, LLM or CNN, has low precision on single niche labels, so the right goal is a short, honest, ranked suggestion rather than an authoritative tag. That changes both the UI (top-k with confidence, labelled as inferred from audio) and the evaluation (your own 50 obscure tracks rather than published GTZAN scores). It also means the non-commercial Discogs model and the permissive CLAP model are closer in practice than their benchmarks suggest, and your own test set should decide between them.
